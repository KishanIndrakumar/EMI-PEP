Clinical Management Summary

Immediate wound care — do this NOW Wash wound with soap and running water for 10–15 minutes. Apply virucidal agent (povidone-iodine solution or 40–70% alcohol). Cover with clean dressing. Do NOT scrub or suck wound. Do NOT delay PET for wound care. Assess tetanus (animal bite = tetanus-prone wound — follow tetanus assessment pathway). Consider antibiotic prophylaxis. Surgical review for severe, deep, hand/facial or head/neck wounds.
URGENT — Head / Neck / Face Wound Wounds to the head, neck and face are highly innervated with direct proximity to cranial nerves — potential incubation period may be as short as 4–5 days. Begin treatment immediately — this requires immediate referral for expert surgical, infectious disease and virological assessment, in addition to starting PET without delay. Give HRIG if indicated by the Composite Rabies Risk (CRR) and the patient's immunisation status. Contact NCCS immediately for HRIG (vaccines@udd.ie). Do not wait for documentation before starting vaccine.
URGENT — Hand / Fingers / Genitals Wound (Red CRR) Hand, finger and genital wounds are highly innervated, high-risk exposure sites. This exposure is Red CRR — start PET (and HRIG if indicated) within 12 hours of the patient presenting (NIAC Ch18 / NHPO V1.2 §5.2.1 high-risk timing rule). Contact NCCS immediately for HRIG (vaccines@udd.ie). Do not wait for documentation before starting vaccine.
Mucosal exposure — automatically set to Category 3 Contamination of mucous membranes (eye, mouth, nose) with animal saliva is Category 3 per NHPO Table 1 (April 2026). The exposure category has been automatically set to Category 3. If no saliva contact with a mucous membrane actually occurred, go back and select a different wound site and exposure category.

R3
Animal type
Bats have specific risk rules — see note below if selected
What type of animal was involved?
Dog / cat — 15-day observation rule (NHPO §4.4.1 / UKHSA B4): If the animal can be observed and remains behaviourally normal for 15 days after the date of exposure, any PET that has been started may be stopped. This applies only if: family-owned pet, provoked bite, owner can closely monitor. If any doubt — start PET and do not delay. Vaccination of a dog or cat does not exclude the need for assessment.
Rodents and primates (NHPO §2.1.2): Monkeys and rats rarely transmit rabies to humans, and rabid monkeys tend to die very quickly — but bites or scratches from these animals still require full assessment. There is no country-risk downgrade for rodent or primate exposures — the standard Composite Rabies Risk matrix (Table 2) applies in full, the same as for any other terrestrial mammal.
⚠ Imported animal — contact Public Health urgently (NHPO §2.3.1). Risk assessment must include: country of origin, vaccination history, how long in this country, and whether bred in captivity or wild-caught. Do not finalise PET without specialist input. Start vaccine immediately if indicated — do not delay for assessment.

Wounds to the head, neck, face, hands and genitals are high-risk due to rich nerve supply and proximity to the CNS. Head and neck wounds may have incubation period as short as 4–5 days (NHPO §4.4.1).

R7
Previous rabies vaccination — PrEP or prior PET (before this exposure)
Vaccination received months or years ago — NOT the treatment started for this current bite or scratch. If in any doubt — treat as unvaccinated.
Has the patient ever received rabies vaccine before this exposure — either as pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP) or as a completed course for a previous, unrelated incident?
Irish definition (NIAC Ch18 / NHPO V1.2): Fully vaccinated = 2 or more documented doses as part of PrEP or a prior PET course, OR documented RVNA titre ≥0.5 IU/ml.
If documentation is absent, incomplete or uncertain — treat as unvaccinated. HRIG is not routinely indicated for partially vaccinated patients unless immunosuppressed.
⚠ This question is about vaccination before this incident. Doses received abroad for this current exposure are asked separately in the next question (R10).
R8
Immunosuppression status
Immunosuppressed patients: 5-dose schedule + HRIG for both Amber and Red CRR
Is the patient immunosuppressed?
Consider immunosuppressed if any of the following (NIAC Ch18 Table 18.5 / NHPO §5.1):
HIV with CD4 <200 cells/µl (adults) or CD4% <15% (children) · haematological malignancy (leukaemia, lymphoma) · solid organ or haematopoietic stem cell transplant · chemotherapy/radiotherapy in past 6 months · high-dose corticosteroids (≥20mg prednisolone/day >10 days in past month) · biologic immunomodulators (TNF inhibitors, B-cell therapies, JAK inhibitors) · primary immunodeficiency · renal dialysis · recent immunosuppressive therapy for solid organ transplant or autoimmune disease.
If uncertain — seek advice from treating clinician. This must not delay immediate administration of vaccine and/or HRIG.
R9
Pregnancy / breastfeeding
Rabies PET is safe and must not be withheld in pregnancy or breastfeeding
Is the patient pregnant or breastfeeding?
Rabies vaccine (Verorab) and HRIG are safe in pregnancy and breastfeeding. PET must never be withheld — the risk of untreated rabies is invariably fatal (NIAC Ch18 §18.5 / NHPO V1.2).
R10
Treatment started abroad for this exposure?
Doses received since this bite/scratch — in the country where it happened. Approximately 80% of patients presenting in Ireland will have started PET at the point of exposure.
Did the patient receive any rabies vaccine doses for this current exposure before returning to Ireland?
If PET abroad used a WHO-recommended vaccine (modern concentrated purified cell culture or embryonated egg-based, ≥2.5 IU/ml per IM dose) and a WHO-recommended regimen — continue/complete Irish schedule. If vaccine type unknown, incompatible, non-WHO schedule, or counterfeit vaccine suspected — restart full course. Refer to NHPO guidance for series completion.
ℹ This question is about doses given after this bite/scratch, abroad. Prior PrEP or previous PET (unrelated to this exposure) was asked in the earlier question (R7).
R11

HRIG and ERIG are given once only. If HRIG/ERIG was confirmed given abroad, do not repeat. If unknown — see result for guidance on precautionary approach.
